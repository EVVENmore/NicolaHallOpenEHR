Reflective Journal 3

OpenEHR and the Digital Adaption Kit for Antenatal Care

An introduction video was developed to support clinical stakeholders who were being newly introduced to openEHR and to the Digital Adaptation Kit. This was necessary because many of the participants were experts in clinical care but had limited prior exposure to health informatics concepts. The video provided a clear and accessible overview, establishing a shared understanding of the key ideas before any detailed discussions or workshops began. By doing so, it helped ensure that all stakeholders started from the same baseline, reducing confusion and improving the quality of subsequent engagement. The video also clarified the role of clinical stakeholders in the process. It emphasized that their contribution was not secondary to technical teams, but central to defining the clinical meaning and structure of the data. This helped shift expectations, making it clear that they were active contributors to the modelling process rather than passive participants. As a result, stakeholders were better prepared to engage in discussions and to understand the importance of their input. In addition, the introduction explained why modelling is a critical step within the DAK approach. While the DAK provides standardized clinical workflows and guidance, it does not directly translate into a functional digital system. The video outlined how modelling using openEHR archetypes and templates enables these guidelines to be represented as structured, computable data. This is essential for ensuring consistency, supporting decision-making, and enabling interoperability across systems. Finally, the video served as a consistent and reusable onboarding resource. It ensured that all participants received the same information in a clear and concise format, and it could be revisited as needed or shared with new stakeholders joining the process at a later stage. This contributed to stronger alignment, improved confidence among participants, and more effective engagement in the modelling activities that followed.

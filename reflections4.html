Reflective Journal 4

Reflecting on a Fellowship Journey

Time has passed swiftly throughout my fellowship, and while I had hoped to achieve every objective set at the outset, I am genuinely satisfied with the progress made. The experience has been invaluable, allowing me to develop new skills, build relationships, and make meaningful contributions to digital health.
Embarking on my openEHR fellowship, I was driven by the ambition to contribute meaningfully to healthcare transformation through the adoption of openEHR standards. This journey unfolded against a backdrop of complex stakeholder landscapes, ambitious technical projects, and a in my corner of the world a significant upheaval in public health due to an unprecedented natural disaster.
Against this backdrop the Matt’s presentation on stakeholder engagement resonated with me. Stakeholder engagement is important, ensuring decision-makers are in the picture early enough, otherwise engagement may not lead to change. This I took for granted because MOHW had actively engaged with openEHR, reached out to person to develop the electronic health record and has for the past eight years been working actively to model forms across various programme areas. The presentation and discussion had me thinking about what I was leading conversations with and if the discourse that had occurred prior had resulted in poisoned fruit discussions where persons were robbed of the discovery process of their wants and needs for the EHR in lieu of the super technical framework. Genuine engagement with key stakeholders was essential for meaningful progress. As I navigated the complexities of aligning technical ambitions with the realities of clinical practice, I found that fostering open dialogue and encouraging active participation were instrumental in bridging gaps between technical teams and healthcare professionals.
After months of reaching out, meeting with the WHO representative was a turning point. Reviewing the DAK materials and engaging with implementers provided invaluable insights into how WHO envisions digital tools being used in practice, and reinforced the importance of stakeholder engagement at every stage. WHO’s involvement highlighted the global significance of interoperability, and their support was instrumental in aligning my efforts with international best practices. However, I was also faced with questions about the necessity of openEHR modelling when interoperability seemed assured based on what was supplied in the DAK. This experience underscored the importance of explaining the technical rigour and the need for embedded open standards, not just terminologies or applications, for true interoperability. This is core to what the openEHR brings to the Global Initiative on Digital Health. By prioritising robust data quality, standardisation, and interoperability, openEHR supports the creation of scalable and adaptable health systems that can be implemented across diverse regions. Its framework not only addresses technical challenges but also facilitates meaningful collaboration among stakeholders, ensuring digital health solutions are fit for purpose and aligned with global priorities.
I was also able to collaborate with the CSIRO team to further enriched the fellowship. Together, we worked on the Pacific Digital Health Interoperability Bootcamp, a regional initiative focused on strengthening digital health, data quality, and interoperability across Pacific Island Countries. This partnership, along with guidance from my mentor, expanded the geographic reach of openEHR and the DAK project. This also went towards fulfilling one of the aims of my fellowship, sharing my experience with other countries that were not as deeply embedded in the openEHR community or tentatively considering involvement.
By integrating openEHR into WHO solutions, the groundwork was laid for transitioning from standalone applications to unified ecosystems, characterised by central clinical data repositories and natively interoperable services. This shift supports longitudinal health records and enables new services, ultimately benefiting clinicians, policy makers, and patients. The fellowship was a profound learning experience, both professionally and personally. Navigating stakeholder engagement taught me the value of enthusiasm and clear communication. Tackling technical challenges honed my analytical and problem-solving abilities. International collaboration fostered adaptability and cultural sensitivity, while working with diverse teams broadened my perspective on global health informatics. Through introductory meetings, presentations, and hands-on modelling, I developed skills in project management, technical translation, and capacity building. The opportunity to work with mentors and international experts not only expanded my knowledge but also inspired a deeper commitment to advancing digital health in my community and beyond.
Reflecting on my fellowship journey, I am grateful for the opportunities to engage with stakeholders, solve complex technical problems, and collaborate with leading organisations. The experience reinforced the importance of aligning digital health innovations with real-world needs, and highlighted the power of open standards like openEHR in driving interoperability and data quality.
As I look ahead, I am optimistic about completing my work with the DAK and localisation for Jamaica. This would be the first WHO DAK template and I am hopeful that this can spur adoption across the other DAKs and data systems developed by the WHO. The lessons learned and partnerships forged during the fellowship will continue to guide my efforts to support sustainable, equitable, and interoperable healthcare systems. The impact of this journey extends beyond my own growth, influencing the broader healthcare community and paving the way for continued progress in openEHR community.
